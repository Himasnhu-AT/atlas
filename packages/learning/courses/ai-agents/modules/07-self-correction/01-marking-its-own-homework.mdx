The cheapest idea in agent engineering is to ask twice. Generate an answer, then ask whether the
answer is right. It costs one extra call and it sounds like it should catch a lot.

Sometimes it does. Often it produces a confident endorsement of a wrong answer, which is worse
than not asking, because now there is a check on the record.

## Why the second look agrees

When you send *"Here is my answer. Is it correct?"*, the model reads the answer, the question,
and usually the reasoning that produced it. Everything that led to the error is still there.

If the error came from a misread number in a document, the misread number is still in the
context. If it came from an ambiguous question, the question is still ambiguous. The second
pass is not a fresh attempt; it is the same attempt with the conclusion already written down,
and a conclusion in the context is a strong hint about what the answer should be.

There is a second effect on top of that: models are agreeable. Asked *"is this right?"* they say
yes more often than the answer deserves, and asked *"this is wrong, isn't it?"* they will
frequently find a fault in a correct answer. The framing moves the verdict, which means the
verdict is partly measuring your framing.

## The property that matters

Every pattern in this module is a way of buying **independence**: making the second look depend
on less of what produced the first.

Rank them by how much they remove:

**Ask again, same context.** Removes nothing. Nearly worthless.

**Ask again with the conclusion hidden.** Sample the same question twice and compare. Removes
the anchor, keeps the context. This is self-consistency, and it works because two independent
attempts at a hard question disagree in a way one attempt cannot reveal.

**Ask about a piece, not the whole.** Decompose the answer into individual claims and check each
one on its own, without the surrounding argument. Removes most of the reasoning that produced
the error. This is chain of verification.

**Check against something outside the model.** Run the code. Query the source document. Compare
the number to the database. Removes the model from the check entirely.

That last one is the strongest and it is not always available, which is why the others exist.
But the ordering is the point: **prefer the check that reads least of what you are checking.**

You have met this argument before. In
[a guardrail that cannot be talked out of](lesson:ai-agent-security.a-guardrail-that-cannot-be-talked-out-of)
it was about attackers, and the conclusion was that a checker reading the poisoned transcript
shares the poisoning. This is the same structure with a different cause: a checker reading the
flawed reasoning shares the flaw. Security and correctness turn out to want the same thing.

## What self-correction cannot fix

Worth saying plainly, because these patterns get sold as more than they are.

**A model that does not know something will not discover it by thinking harder.** If the answer
depends on a fact absent from the context, no number of passes retrieves it. The fix is
retrieval, which is the last lesson of this module.

**Reflection cannot rescue a bad plan.** If the approach was wrong, critiquing the output tends
to produce a better-written version of the same wrong thing.

**More passes are not monotonically better.** Loops that revise until satisfied can drift:
each pass makes a small change to please the critic, and after four passes the answer has moved
away from the question. Bounded loops, always.

## The cost, which is the reason to be selective

Every pattern here multiplies calls. Chain of verification on a five-claim answer is six calls
instead of one. Self-consistency at n=5 is five. Both together is thirty.

At the sizes in this course that is nothing. In production it is your bill and your latency, and
`run_ledger` from
[observability and cost](lesson:ai-multi-agent-and-production.observability-and-cost) is what
tells you whether the accuracy you bought was worth it.

The honest default is to apply verification to the things that are expensive to get wrong and
to leave everything else alone. A wrong summary is a nuisance. A wrong citation in a document
somebody publishes is a different category, which is why the
[research briefing](lesson:ai-research-briefing.the-verification-pass) project verifies claims
and does not verify prose.

## What this module builds

Reflection and its failure mode. Chain of verification, which is the general form of the
project's verification pass. Self-consistency, and using disagreement as a confidence signal.
Then retrieval that grades its own results and re-queries when they do not support the
question, which is the same idea applied to the part of the system that actually can go and
find the missing fact.
