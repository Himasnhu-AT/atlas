The Black-Scholes formula applies only to European options that cannot be exercised prior to expiration.

Most listed equity options are **American options**, which allow the holder to exercise early at any date up to maturity. Because early exercise creates an optimal stopping problem with no closed-form analytical solution, numerical lattices such as the **Cox-Ross-Rubinstein (CRR) Binomial Tree** are used.

## The CRR lattice parameters

Over discrete time step $\Delta t = \frac{\tau}{N}$:

1. Up factor: $u = e^{\sigma \sqrt{\Delta t}}$
2. Down factor: $d = \frac{1}{u} = e^{-\sigma \sqrt{\Delta t}}$
3. Risk-neutral up probability:

$$
p = \frac{e^{r \Delta t} - d}{u - d}
$$

## Backward induction and early exercise

Pricing proceeds in reverse from expiration step $N$ back to step 0:

1. **Terminal payoffs at step $N$**: For each node $j \in [0, N]$:

$$
S_{N, j} = S_0 \cdot u^j \cdot d^{N - j}
$$

Option values are initialized to intrinsic value: $V_{N, j} = \max(0, S_{N, j} - K)$ for calls.

2. **Backward induction from step $i = N-1$ down to 0**:
   At each node $(i, j)$, calculate the expected discounted continuation value:

$$
V_{\text{continuation}} = e^{-r \Delta t} \left[ p V_{i+1, j+1} + (1 - p) V_{i+1, j} \right]
$$

For an **American option**, the holder exercises if the immediate intrinsic value exceeds continuation value:

$$
V_{i, j} = \max\left( V_{\text{continuation}}, \; \text{Intrinsic}(S_{i, j}) \right)
$$

For a European option, $V_{i, j} = V_{\text{continuation}}$.

## Memory efficiency in Python

A full 2D tree stores $\frac{(N+1)(N+2)}{2}$ nodes in $O(N^2)$ space. By updating a single 1D array of size $N+1$ in place during backward sweeps, space complexity reduces to $O(N)$.
