To manage derivative portfolios, quant desks decompose price risk into partial derivatives known as **the Greeks**.

## The fundamental sensitivities

For an option price $V(S, \sigma, r, \tau)$:

1. **Delta ($\Delta = \frac{\partial V}{\partial S}$)**:
   The change in option price per \$1 change in underlying spot price.
   * Call: $\Delta_C = \Phi(d_1) \in (0, 1)$
   * Put: $\Delta_P = -\Phi(-d_1) = \Phi(d_1) - 1 \in (-1, 0)$
   A portfolio with $\Delta = 0$ is **delta-neutral**.

2. **Gamma ($\Gamma = \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2} = \frac{\partial \Delta}{\partial S}$)**:
   The rate of change of delta per \$1 move in spot price. Gamma measures the curvature and hedging error of a linear delta hedge. Identical for calls and puts:

$$
\Gamma = \frac{\phi(d_1)}{S \sigma \sqrt{\tau}}
$$

3. **Vega ($\mathcal{V} = \frac{\partial V}{\partial \sigma}$)**:
   The sensitivity of the option price to a change in implied volatility. By market convention, Vega is reported per 1% (0.01) change in volatility:

$$
\mathcal{V}_{\text{1\%}} = \frac{S \phi(d_1) \sqrt{\tau}}{100}
$$

4. **Theta ($\Theta = -\frac{\partial V}{\partial \tau}$)**:
   The rate of time decay as expiration approaches, reported per 1 calendar day ($\Theta / 365$):

$$
\Theta_C = \frac{1}{365} \left( -\frac{S \phi(d_1) \sigma}{2 \sqrt{\tau}} - r K e^{-r \tau} \Phi(d_2) \right)
$$

5. **Rho ($\rho = \frac{\partial V}{\partial r}$)**:
   Sensitivity to a 1% shift in the risk-free interest rate: $\rho_C = \frac{K \tau e^{-r \tau} \Phi(d_2)}{100}$.
