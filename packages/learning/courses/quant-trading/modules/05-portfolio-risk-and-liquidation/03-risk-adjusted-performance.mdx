Total return alone does not indicate investment quality. A portfolio generating 15% with 5% volatility demonstrates higher skill and leverage capability than a portfolio generating 15% with 25% volatility.

## The annualized Sharpe ratio

The **Sharpe ratio** measures excess return above the risk-free rate per unit of total return volatility:

$$
\text{Sharpe} = \frac{\bar{R} - R_f}{\sigma_R} \sqrt{A}
$$

Where:
* $\bar{R}$ is the average periodic return.
* $R_f$ is the periodic risk-free rate.
* $\sigma_R$ is the sample standard deviation of periodic returns.
* $A$ is the annualization factor (e.g. 252 for daily returns, 52 for weekly returns).

## The Sortino ratio and downside deviation

The Sharpe ratio penalizes upside volatility and downside volatility equally. A strategy with occasional large positive windfall returns is penalized with a lower Sharpe ratio because upside surprises increase standard deviation.

The **Sortino ratio** replaces total volatility with **downside deviation** measured relative to a Minimum Acceptable Return (MAR, often 0.0):

$$
\text{Sortino} = \frac{\bar{R} - \text{MAR}}{\sigma_{\text{downside}}} \sqrt{A}
$$

Where downside semi-variance only accumulates negative deviations:

$$
\sigma_{\text{downside}} = \sqrt{ \frac{1}{N} \sum_{t=1}^N \min(0, \; R_t - \text{MAR})^2 }
$$
