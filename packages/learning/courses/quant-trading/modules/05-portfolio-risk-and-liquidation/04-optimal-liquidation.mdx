Liquidating a large position of $X$ shares presents a fundamental trade-off:
* **Liquidating rapidly** consumes limit order book liquidity, causing severe temporary and permanent market impact.
* **Liquidating slowly** reduces price impact, but exposes the remaining unhedged inventory to market volatility risk.

## The Almgren-Chriss framework

The **Almgren-Chriss model** finds the execution schedule that minimizes expected transaction cost plus a penalty for variance risk.

For total shares $X$, $N$ discrete time intervals of length $\tau$ (total time $T = N \tau$), asset volatility $\sigma$, temporary impact coefficient $\eta$, and trader risk aversion $\lambda$:

The urgency parameter $\kappa$ is:

$$
\kappa \approx \sqrt{\frac{\lambda \sigma^2}{\eta}}
$$

The optimal remaining position $x_k$ at time step $k \in [0, N]$ is:

$$
x_k = X \cdot \frac{\sinh(\kappa (T - k \tau))}{\sinh(\kappa T)}
$$

## Limiting cases

1. **Risk-neutral trader ($\lambda \to 0$ or $\kappa \to 0$)**:
   The trajectory simplifies to a straight line (Time-Weighted Average Price / TWAP):

$$
x_k = X \cdot \left(1 - \frac{k}{N}\right)
$$

2. **Highly risk-averse trader ($\lambda \to \infty$)**:
   The trajectory decays aggressively in early periods to extinguish inventory risk immediately, accepting higher initial impact costs.
