A strategy can generate a 30% annualized return while subjecting the investor to a 60% peak-to-trough decline that takes three years to recover.

Quant risk management quantifies both the depth of loss and the time spent recovering capital.

## High-water marks and drawdown depth

At step $t$, the **high-water mark (peak)** is the highest equity achieved up to that point:

$$
M_t = \max_{0 \le s \le t} E_s
$$

The **drawdown** $\text{DD}_t$ is the percentage decline from the peak:

$$
\text{DD}_t = \frac{M_t - E_t}{M_t}
$$

The **Maximum Drawdown (MDD)** is the worst peak-to-trough decline observed over the entire life of the strategy:

$$
\text{MDD} = \max_{0 \le t \le T} \text{DD}_t
$$

## Drawdown duration (time underwater)

Drawdown depth measures severity; **drawdown duration** measures the psychological and financial endurance required to trade the strategy.

The duration is the number of time intervals elapsed between establishing a peak high-water mark and either setting a new peak or reaching the end of the evaluation window:

$$
\text{Duration}_t = t - \text{argmax}_{0 \le s \le t} E_s
$$

A strategy that suffers a modest 10% drawdown but remains underwater for 500 consecutive trading days often causes investors to liquidate right before a recovery.
