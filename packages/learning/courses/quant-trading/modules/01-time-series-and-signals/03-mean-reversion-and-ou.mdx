A trending strategy profits when price moves persist. A mean-reverting strategy profits when deviations from an equilibrium price decay back toward a long-term average.

## The continuous Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process

The continuous stochastic differential equation for an **Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU)** process is:

$$
\mathrm{d}X_t = \theta (\mu - X_t) \mathrm{d}t + \sigma \mathrm{d}W_t
$$

Where:
* $\theta > 0$ is the rate of mean reversion.
* $\mu$ is the long-term equilibrium mean.
* $\sigma > 0$ is the instantaneous volatility.
* $W_t$ is a standard Brownian motion.

When $X_t > \mu$, the drift term $\theta (\mu - X_t)$ is negative, pulling the process downward. When $X_t < \mu$, the drift is positive, pushing the process upward.

## Discrete calibration via AR(1) regression

Discretizing the OU process over time step $\Delta t$ yields a linear autoregressive relationship of lag 1 (AR(1)):

$$
X_t = a + b X_{t-1} + \epsilon_t
$$

Using Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression of $X_t$ against $X_{t-1}$:

$$
b = \frac{N \sum X_{t-1} X_t - \sum X_{t-1} \sum X_t}{N \sum X_{t-1}^2 - (\sum X_{t-1})^2}, \quad a = \bar{X}_t - b \bar{X}_{t-1}
$$

The continuous parameters are recovered from the regression coefficients:

$$
\theta = -\frac{\ln(b)}{\Delta t}, \quad \mu = \frac{a}{1 - b}
$$

The residual sample variance $s_e^2 = \frac{1}{N-2}\sum (X_t - (a + b X_{t-1}))^2$ provides the continuous diffusion coefficient:

$$
\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{s_e^2 \cdot 2 \theta}{1 - b^2}}
$$

## The half-life of mean reversion

The **half-life** $t_{1/2}$ is the expected time required for the process to decay halfway back to its long-term mean from a displaced state:

$$
t_{1/2} = \frac{\ln(2)}{\theta}
$$

If the estimated slope $b \ge 1.0$, $\theta \le 0$, indicating a random walk or explosive process that does not mean-revert. If $b \le 0$, the process oscillates each step without continuous drift.
