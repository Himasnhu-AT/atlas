Trading systems process continuous market data tick-by-tick. Recalculating mean and variance over a full array on every new tick requires $O(W)$ time per event, where $W$ is the window length.

Online streaming algorithms maintain rolling statistics in $O(1)$ time per update.

## Exponential moving average with warmup

The **Exponential Moving Average (EMA)** assigns exponentially decaying weights to older observations. For a span of $N$ periods, the smoothing multiplier $\alpha$ is:

$$
\alpha = \frac{2}{N + 1}
$$

A common error in naive implementations is initializing $\text{EMA}_0 = 0.0$, which distorts the first dozen values toward zero. A clean approach calculates the simple arithmetic average over the initial warmup window ($t < N$) before applying the recursive smoothing formula:

$$
\text{EMA}_t = \alpha P_t + (1 - \alpha) \text{EMA}_{t-1}
$$

## Sliding window variance and numerical stability

Computing sample variance as $\frac{\sum X^2 - (\sum X)^2 / N}{N - 1}$ in floating point arithmetic can cause catastrophic cancellation when numbers are large and differences are small, sometimes resulting in negative values.

To maintain precision:

1. Maintain running sums of $X$ and $X^2$ over a queue of size $W$.
2. Pop the oldest observation when the queue exceeds $W$.
3. Compute the sample variance:

$$
s^2 = \max\left(0.0, \; \frac{\sum X^2 - N \bar{X}^2}{N - 1}\right)
$$

4. Calculate annualized volatility using the periods per year scaling factor (for daily returns, $A = 252$):

$$
\sigma_{\text{annualized}} = \sqrt{s^2 \cdot A}
$$
