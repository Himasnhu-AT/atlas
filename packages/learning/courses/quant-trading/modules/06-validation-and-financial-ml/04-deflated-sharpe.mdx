When a quant researcher tests 1,000 parameter variations on historical price data, several variations will produce Sharpe ratios exceeding 2.0 purely by random chance (data snooping / selection bias).

Presenting the best Sharpe ratio without adjusting for the number of discarded trials guarantees live performance failure.

## The distribution of the maximum Sharpe ratio

Under the null hypothesis of zero true predictive skill, the expected maximum false-positive Sharpe ratio observed across $N$ independent trials is:

$$
\text{SR}^* \approx \sqrt{2 \ln(N)} + \frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{2 \ln(N)}}
$$

Where $\gamma \approx 0.5772$ is the Euler-Mascheroni constant (for $N=1$, $\text{SR}^* = 0$).

Testing 1,000 variations yields an expected false-positive maximum Sharpe of $\approx 2.4$ solely from noise.

## The Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR)

The **Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR)** computes the probability that the observed Sharpe ratio $\widehat{\text{SR}}$ exceeds the expected maximum noise benchmark $\text{SR}^*$, adjusting for sample length $T$, return skewness $\gamma_3$, and kurtosis $\gamma_4$:

$$
\sigma_{\text{SR}} = \sqrt{\frac{1 - \gamma_3 \widehat{\text{SR}} + \frac{\gamma_4 - 1}{4} \widehat{\text{SR}}^2}{T - 1}}
$$

$$
Z = \frac{\widehat{\text{SR}} - \text{SR}^*}{\sigma_{\text{SR}}}
$$

$$
\text{DSR} = \Phi(Z)
$$

A DSR value exceeding 0.95 (95% statistical confidence) indicates that the strategy's performance cannot be explained by multiple testing.
