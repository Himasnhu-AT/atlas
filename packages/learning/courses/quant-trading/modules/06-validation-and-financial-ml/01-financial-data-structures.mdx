Standard financial analysis aggregates transactions into fixed time bars (e.g. 1-minute, 5-minute, or 1-day OHLCV candles).

Time bars present structural flaws:
1. **Uneven information arrival**: Markets process vast transaction volumes during market open and macroeconomic announcements, but trade thinly during midday lulls. Fixed time bars sample noise during quiet periods and over-compress information during turbulent bursts.
2. **Fat tails and heteroskedasticity**: Return distributions computed from time bars exhibit severe kurtosis (fat tails) and time-varying variance, violating standard regression assumptions.

## Information-driven dollar bars

**Dollar bars** sample market observations whenever a fixed aggregate dollar volume has transacted:

$$
\sum_{k=1}^M P_k \cdot V_k \ge \Theta_{\text{dollar}}
$$

Sampling by dollar value provides three properties:
* The number of bars generated scales dynamically with market activity.
* Return series generated from dollar bars exhibit properties closer to normal (Gaussian) distributions with stable variance.
* The sampling rate automatically adjusts for long-term price drift and stock splits.
