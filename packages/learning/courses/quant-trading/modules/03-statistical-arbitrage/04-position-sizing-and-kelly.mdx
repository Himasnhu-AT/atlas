A trading strategy with a positive expected return can still suffer capital wipeout if position sizes are too large.

The **Kelly criterion** determines the optimal fraction of capital to risk per trade to maximize the long-term compounding growth rate of wealth.

## The discrete Kelly formula

For a strategy with win probability $p$ and win-to-loss payoff ratio $b = \frac{\text{Average Win}}{\text{Average Loss}}$:

$$
f^* = \frac{p(b + 1) - 1}{b} = p - \frac{1 - p}{b}
$$

If $f^* \le 0$, the strategy has negative expected value, and the optimal position size is zero.

## Full Kelly versus Fractional Kelly

In theory, Full Kelly ($1.0 \cdot f^*$) achieves maximum wealth growth over an infinite horizon. In practice, Full Kelly presents two severe problems:

1. **Parameter estimation error**: The true win rate $p$ and payoff ratio $b$ are unknown and estimated from noisy historical samples. Overestimating $p$ leads to over-betting.
2. **Extreme drawdowns**: Full Kelly paths frequently experience peak-to-trough drawdowns exceeding 50% to 80%.

Institutional practitioners use **Half-Kelly** or **Fractional Kelly** ($0.5 \cdot f^*$ or $0.25 \cdot f^*$):
* Sizing at 50% of Full Kelly achieves 75% of the maximum theoretical growth rate.
* It reduces return variance by 75% and dramatically diminishes the probability of catastrophic drawdown.
