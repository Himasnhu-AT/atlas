Time-series strategies trade one asset against its own history. **Cross-sectional factor strategies** rank an entire universe of assets simultaneously at timestamp $t$.

By buying the highest-ranked assets and selling short the lowest-ranked assets, the portfolio eliminates broad market direction (beta), profiting solely from the relative outperformance of the factor.

## Dollar neutrality and gross exposure

A **dollar-neutral portfolio** balances aggregate long notional value against short notional value:

$$
\sum_{i=1}^M w_{\text{long}, i} = +0.5, \quad \sum_{j=1}^M w_{\text{short}, j} = -0.5
$$

Net market exposure sums to zero:

$$
\text{Net Exposure} = \sum_{k=1}^N w_k = 0.0
$$

Gross market exposure sums to full capital utilization:

$$
\text{Gross Exposure} = \sum_{k=1}^N |w_k| = 1.0
$$

If the broader stock market crashes by 20% overnight, a perfectly dollar-neutral portfolio experiences approximately zero direct market beta loss, as short gains offset long losses.

## Ranking and quantile selection

At each rebalancing interval:
1. Compute the raw factor metric (e.g. 12-month momentum, book-to-market value, earnings quality) for every asset in the universe.
2. Sort the universe in descending order of factor score.
3. Allocate positive weight equally across the top $K$ assets.
4. Allocate negative weight equally across the bottom $K$ assets.
5. Set all middle assets to zero weight.
