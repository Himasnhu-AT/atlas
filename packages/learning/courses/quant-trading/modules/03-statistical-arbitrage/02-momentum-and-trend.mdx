**Time-series momentum** (trend following) assumes that an asset with positive excess returns over a lookback window will continue to outperform over future horizons.

Unscaled momentum strategies suffer from extreme tail risk: when market volatility surges during market crashes, unadjusted positions generate massive drawdowns.

## Volatility scaling

To equalize risk exposure across calm and turbulent regimes, quantitative trend strategies scale position sizes inversely by realized volatility:

$$
w_t = \text{sign}(R_{\text{lookback}}) \times \min\left(1.0, \; \frac{\sigma_{\text{target}}}{\sigma_{\text{realized}}}\right)
$$

Where:
* $R_{\text{lookback}} = \frac{P_t - P_{t-N}}{P_{t-N}}$ is the lookback return over $N$ periods.
* $\sigma_{\text{realized}} = \sqrt{252} \cdot \text{std}(r_{t-N \to t})$ is the annualized realized volatility over the same lookback.
* $\sigma_{\text{target}}$ is the annualized target risk (e.g. 15% or 0.15).

When market volatility is high ($\sigma_{\text{realized}} > \sigma_{\text{target}}$), position size contracts to protect capital. When volatility is subdued, position size expands toward the 1.0 leverage cap.
