A pairs trading strategy monetizes temporary deviations between two cointegrated assets $Y$ and $X$.

When the spread residual $e_t = Y_t - (\alpha + \beta X_t)$ diverges from zero, the strategy opens a market-neutral position and closes it once the spread returns to equilibrium.

## Signal thresholds and state transitions

Using the normalized spread z-score $Z_t = \frac{e_t - \mu_e}{\sigma_e}$, a discrete state machine governs trading decisions:

1. **Entry conditions (from flat)**:
   * If $Z_t \le -Z_{\text{entry}}$ (e.g. -2.0): $Y$ is underpriced relative to $X$. Enter **Long Spread** (Long $Y$, Short $\beta$ of $X$). Position state becomes `+1`.
   * If $Z_t \ge +Z_{\text{entry}}$ (e.g. +2.0): $Y$ is overpriced relative to $X$. Enter **Short Spread** (Short $Y$, Long $\beta$ of $X$). Position state becomes `-1`.

2. **Exit on convergence**:
   * For Long Spread (`+1`): Exit to flat (`0`) when $Z_t \ge -Z_{\text{exit}}$ (e.g. -0.5).
   * For Short Spread (`-1`): Exit to flat (`0`) when $Z_t \le +Z_{\text{exit}}$ (e.g. +0.5).

3. **Stop-loss on structural divergence**:
   * If $Z_t$ continues widening past $\pm Z_{\text{stop}}$ (e.g. $\pm 3.5$), the cointegration relationship may have broken. The strategy closes all positions immediately to avoid unbounded loss.
